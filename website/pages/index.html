A room of instruments, heard from where you stand.

StudioSim is a music studio you walk around in. Put instruments in a room, play them, mic them up, then record and mix the way a DAW works. Everything you hear is rendered for headphones from where you stand and which way you face.

A room, a band's worth of instruments, and a workstation

A room that sounds like one

Set its size, stand walls in it and choose what every surface is made of. Sound bends round walls, leaks through them and reflects off them, and the reverb follows the room.

Instruments to play

Drum kits, synths, keys, tubular bells, guitar and bass amps with pedalboards, and your own plug-in instruments. Play them from the computer keyboard or a MIDI controller.

A workstation to record in

A timeline, takes and comping, clip editing, automation, and a mix window with inserts, sends, folders and aux tracks, laid out as Pro Tools users expect.

Other people to play with

Host your room and others join it over your network or the internet. Everyone hears it from where they stand, in time on a shared clock.

You hear where you are

Walk up to the drums and they get louder and closer. Turn your head and the room turns with it. Step behind a wall and the highs fall away. StudioSim works out how each instrument's sound reaches your ears through the room and renders it binaurally, so on headphones every sound has a place.

More about the room

Mic it like a studio

Microphones hear the room from where they stand. Choose a model and a pattern, point it, and look down its capsule to see what it sees. Put a dynamic on an amp's cone or mic a whole kit in one go, and each mic gets a track of its own to record.

More about microphones

Record and mix like a DAW

Arm tracks, record takes, comp them, edit clips and draw automation. The mix window has inserts for your plug-ins, sends to aux tracks, routing folders and delay compensation, with the room and the mix as two masters, so you can hear the players' room or your mix of it.
